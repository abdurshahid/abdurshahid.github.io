This program investigates how human interactions with generative AI systems may expose sensitive behavioral, health, identity, and contextual information through writing, sketches, whiteboard content, audio, images, and other multimodal signals.

We evaluate privacy claims made by consumer AI tools and develop lightweight, user-centered privacy techniques that can operate on smartphones, wearables, and resource-constrained devices before information is shared with cloud-hosted models.

A major current effort is a large-scale multilingual benchmark for on-device generative image editing across consumer platforms, devices, AI models, editing operations, and JPEG AI compression conditions. The benchmark is intended to support research in multimedia forensics, computer vision, multimodal grounding, edit localization, content provenance, and trustworthy consumer AI.
