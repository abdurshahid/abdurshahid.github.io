I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Southern Illinois University Carbondale and the founder and director of the Secure and Trustworthy Intelligent Systems (SHIELD) Lab. My research focuses on the security, privacy, trustworthiness, and sustainability of intelligent systems, with particular emphasis on multimodal and generative AI, wearable and edge intelligence, federated learning, cyber-physical systems, and digital forensics.

Before entering academia, I worked as a software engineer at Samsung R&D Institute Bangladesh and Samsung Electronics in South Korea, where I contributed to Android software for commercial mobile devices. This product-development background continues to shape my interest in practical, deployable, and resource-aware intelligent systems.

I currently advise three Ph.D. students and two master’s students, in addition to undergraduate researchers and external collaborators. Our work develops datasets, benchmarks, attack frameworks, privacy-preserving methods, and system prototypes that connect foundational research with real-world applications.

Research programs

  • Secure Multimodal Sensing–Language Systems — security- and privacy-aware architectures integrating sensors, wearables, edge devices, vision-language models, and large or small language models.
  • Privacy and Trust in Human–Generative AI Interaction — behavioral privacy, inference risks, consumer AI tools, lightweight on-device privacy, generative AI forensics, and provenance.
  • Adversarial and Resource-Aware AI Security — poisoning, cross-modal attacks, adversarial VLMs, energy-latency attacks, defenses, and security–sustainability trade-offs.
  • Privacy-Preserving Distributed and Edge Intelligence — federated learning, differential privacy, lightweight IoT security, and resource-efficient decentralized intelligence.

I welcome collaborations with researchers and students working on trustworthy AI, AI security, privacy, cyber-physical systems, and multimodal computing.
